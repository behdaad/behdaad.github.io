The events

CONTEXT BEFORE EACH WATCH

Dictatorship and rearmament

Hitler became chancellor in 1933 and dismantled German democracy. Rearmament challenged the post-WWI order. The Gathering Storm approaches these dangers through Churchill’s warnings and domestic difficulties in Britain; it is background to Nazi expansion, not a history of Hitler’s rise.

USHMM · Nazi rise to power ↗

The war in Asia is already underway

Full-scale war between China and Japan began in 1937, years before Pearl Harbor. Japanese forces captured Nanjing that December and committed mass atrocities against civilians and prisoners. City of Life and Death places that violence at the centre of the route.

Munich and the limits of appeasement

Britain and France accepted Germany’s annexation of the Sudetenland at Munich, under pressure from Hitler and without Czechoslovakia at the negotiations. Read this after The Gathering Storm: its central story ends earlier and its 1939 coda does not fill in Munich.

Poland is invaded; Europe goes to war

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September; Britain and France declared war two days later. The Soviet Union invaded from the east on 17 September. Schindler’s List begins in the resulting German occupation, rather than reconstructing the invasion itself.

03Schindler’s ListOccupation, not invasion↗

Supply lines are a battlefield

German U-boats attacked the merchant shipping on which Britain and the Allied war effort depended. Escorts, intelligence, aircraft and industrial production gradually changed the balance. Das Boot follows the German side of one patrol in 1941, not the entire Atlantic campaign.

05Das BootWatch for this event↗
US Navy Museum · Battle of the Atlantic ↗

France falls; an army escapes

Germany’s western offensive trapped Allied armies near Dunkirk. An evacuation saved roughly 338,000 troops, but left equipment behind and did not prevent France’s defeat. Dunkirk makes the escape immediate; its interwoven time scales all remain within 1940.

04DunkirkWatch for this event↗
USHMM · Dunkirk evacuation ↗

The battle moves into the air

The Luftwaffe failed to gain the air superiority needed for a German invasion of Britain. Radar, organisation, aircraft production and aircrew from several countries mattered alongside the pilots. Dunkirk supplies an earlier view of RAF combat, not a depiction of this later air campaign.

04DunkirkEarlier air-war context↗
Royal Air Force · Battle of Britain ↗

Hitler attacks the Soviet Union

Operation Barbarossa opened an immense eastern war of conquest and annihilation, accompanied by mass murder. The invasion failed to secure a quick victory. Read this before Enemy at the Gates, which begins later at Stalingrad rather than depicting Barbarossa.

08Enemy at the GatesLater Eastern Front context↗
USHMM · Invasion of the Soviet Union ↗

Persecution becomes systematic genocide

Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered six million Jews. War, occupation, mass shootings, deportations and killing centres were integral to this genocide. Schindler’s List follows persecution, forced labour and rescue around Kraków; it is one story within a much larger crime.

03Schindler’s ListWatch for this event↗
USHMM · War and mass murder ↗

Pearl Harbor makes the war global

Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on 7 December as it launched wider offensives in Asia and the Pacific. The United States entered the war; Germany and Italy then declared war on it. Tora! Tora! Tora! follows Japanese planning and American failures before the attack.

The Pacific initiative begins to shift

US carrier aircraft sank four Japanese carriers at Midway in June. The Guadalcanal campaign began in August, opening a long struggle on land, at sea and in the air. The Pacific follows Marines at Guadalcanal; it does not show the carrier battle at Midway.

07The PacificGuadalcanal, not Midway↗

Stalingrad reverses the eastern campaign

The battle for Stalingrad consumed German and Soviet armies. Soviet encirclement trapped the German Sixth Army, which surrendered in early 1943. Enemy at the Gates uses a heavily dramatised sniper rivalry to enter this battle; it is not an account of the whole Soviet victory.

08Enemy at the GatesWatch for this event↗
National WWII Museum · Stalingrad ↗

The Allies gain ground in North Africa

Victory at El Alamein and the Anglo-American Torch landings squeezed Axis forces between armies advancing from east and west. The remaining Axis forces surrendered in Tunisia in May 1943. This secured the next stage of the Mediterranean campaign. Patton joins the story in Tunisia in 1943, after El Alamein and Torch.

09PattonTunisia in 1943↗
National Army Museum · North Africa ↗

Italy is not a shortcut

The Allies invaded Sicily and mainland Italy in 1943. Mussolini fell and Italy signed an armistice, but German occupation and difficult terrain prolonged the fighting. The campaign continued alongside the later Normandy offensive, rather than ending when D-Day began. Patton depicts the Sicilian stage.

The western Allies return to France

D-Day opened the Normandy landings, followed by a hard-fought campaign inland. Band of Brothers follows one US airborne company from the night drop into Normandy and onward. Its viewpoint is narrower than the full multinational invasion.

The advance stalls and counterattacks

The attempt to cross the Rhine through the Netherlands failed at Arnhem. Germany then attacked in the Ardennes, producing the Battle of the Bulge. Band of Brothers covers Market Garden and the fighting around Bastogne, showing why victory was not immediate after Normandy. Patton adds an army-command perspective on the relief of Bastogne.

The island campaigns grow more costly

Peleliu, Iwo Jima and Okinawa reveal different stages of the advance towards Japan. The Pacific follows Marines through these battles, including encounters with civilians at Okinawa. It is a ground-level account, not a complete explanation of naval or air strategy.

Camps are liberated; Germany collapses

Soviet and western Allied armies liberated camps as they entered German-held territory. Soviet forces took Berlin; Germany surrendered in May. Downfall portrays the regime’s final days. Band of Brothers includes a camp liberation, while Schindler’s List reaches the end of its rescue story.

Bombing, Soviet entry and Japan’s surrender

Conventional bombing devastated Japanese cities. The US atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and Soviet entry into the war in August preceded Japan’s surrender announcement on 15 August and formal signing on 2 September. Grave of the Fireflies shows civilian survival after Kobe’s firebombing, not the atomic attacks.

07The PacificSurrender news and aftermath↗12Grave of the FirefliesKobe civilians, not atomic attacks↗

The atomic bombings and Soviet offensive are essential context, but are not directly reconstructed by these selections. The Pacific includes news of surrender and its consequences.

Home is not an instant recovery

Demobilisation brought relief alongside grief, displacement and lasting trauma. The Pacific’s final episode spends substantial time with veterans trying to resume civilian life, including Eugene Sledge’s 1946 homecoming. That is why its bar extends beyond the fighting in 1945.

Dunkirk

Soldiers on a beach, civilians in a small boat and RAF pilots converge during the evacuation from France. Three different time scales make the rescue feel like a race.

Story dates & historical context

May–June 1940 only. The film intercuts a week on land, a day at sea and an hour in the air; these are overlapping durations within the same operation, not separate story years. Characters and incidents are largely fictionalised.

HOW TO READ THIS

Stories overlap.
History keeps moving.

Story dates and release dates are different things. A span tells you when the main plot takes place; it does not mean every event in those years appears on screen. Links marked “Context” or “Background” explain connections beyond a film’s depicted events.

A gap means a gap: separate story periods stay separate. Viewing numbers suggest an order, even when their years overlap. Sources and important dramatic liberties sit under each title’s story-date notes.

The rest of the picture

The route connects Europe and the Pacific with China, the Holocaust, naval warfare and civilian survival. It is still selective: North Africa, Italy, resistance movements, the wider Eastern Front, Southeast Asia and the colonial contribution need more than these twelve titles. Essential gaps are explained in the event notes, without implying that a film depicts them.

Germany surrendered in May 1945; Japan announced surrender in August and signed on 2 September. The chart extends into 1946 because The Pacific devotes a substantial final episode to homecoming and readjustment, not merely an old-age epilogue. Films are selected for engaging viewing, not as substitutes for the historical sources.
