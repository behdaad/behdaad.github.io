# Space exploration: research and curation

Research date: 30 October 2026. Content lives in `../data.json`; `../index.html` is generated with `python3 war-timeline/build.py space-exploration`.

## Scope and route

The requested format includes films, series and documentaries. Eight selections give a route from test flying and Mercury through Apollo, Voyager, the Shuttle and the Mars rovers. The films *First Man* and *Apollo 13* are optional close-ups alongside the much broader *From the Earth to the Moon*, rather than eight compulsory consecutive lessons. No streaming availability in the Netherlands has been verified.

## Coverage decisions and uncertainties

- **The Right Stuff:** [AFI's detailed synopsis](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/58061-THE-RIGHT-STUFF) dates Yeager's X-1 sequence to 1947, its next test-pilot chapter to 1952 and Cooper's final Mercury flight to 1963. The substantial 1947 chapter remains visible; 1948–1951 are left empty. This uses AFI's dating of the intervening chapter rather than claiming every year appears on screen. AFI's wording mistakenly calls Glenn's flight the world's first orbital mission; the page explicitly distinguishes Gagarin's earlier achievement. The hatch scene's treatment of Grissom is not evidence that he panicked; [NASA's mission record](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/mercury-redstone-4-liberty-bell-7/) describes a hatch problem. The [Smithsonian's First Man assessment](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/what-first-man-got-right-about-neil-armstrong) also discusses misleading dramatic treatment of Glenn's mission in the older film.
- **Hidden Figures:** NASA's film announcement identifies the 1962 Glenn mission. The film concentrates its central action in 1961–1962. Its childhood prologue is excluded, and real career dates must not be confused with the film's compressed chronology: [Vaughan was a supervisor from 1949](https://www.nasa.gov/people/dorothy-vaughan/), and [Jackson became an engineer in 1958](https://www.nasa.gov/history/mary-w-jackson/). Those dates are evidence of compression, not additional story coverage.
- **First Man:** The Smithsonian explicitly describes the film's scope as 1961–1969. Personal scenes interpret Armstrong, and the lunar bracelet scene is unverified. Its 1965 connection introduces Gemini and Leonov's milestone; it does not claim Armstrong made the first spacewalk.
- **From the Earth to the Moon:** [AFI's series description](https://watch.afi.com/movie/tv-series-from-the-earth-to-the-moon) gives 1961–1972; IMDb confirms twelve episodes. Its 1902 Méliès material is a parallel artistic frame, not an Apollo-era interval. Episode references identify a useful route through specific milestones; the recommendation and time budget include the full series.
- **Apollo 13:** The main mission narrative is 1970. The Apollo 1 framing and the Apollo 11 television celebration are background rather than an expanded 1967–1970 bar. NASA's flight record anchors the mission; the Smithsonian's [discussion with Jeffrey Kluger and Margaret Weitekamp](https://airandspace.si.edu/multimedia-gallery/apollo-13-film-discussion) addresses the film's relationship to history.
- **The Farthest:** [PBS supplies a transcript](https://www.pbs.org/video/the-farthest-voyager-in-space-qpbu4y/) explicitly dating preparation to 1972, the launch to 1977, and the heliopause crossing to 2012. This is a historical subject envelope with jumps, not an assertion of continuous year-by-year footage. The feature runtime is 121 minutes on IMDb; PBS's retrieved cut is 97 minutes 12 seconds. Both are identified so their runtimes are not conflated. Publicity's “left the solar system” is qualified against NASA's heliosphere definition. Interviews recorded for the 2017 film do not extend the bar to 2017.
- **Challenger:** Netflix describes the program, launch warnings, loss and investigation. The editorial 1978 boundary represents the astronaut class and its place in the series' main historical story, rather than the start of every archival sequence. Earlier program footage and later remembrance do not enlarge the span. The 1988 return to flight is not treated as a substantial extra chapter. The page clearly distinguishes Challenger in 1986 from Columbia in 2003.
- **Good Night Oppy:** Approximate development-to-end-of-mission coverage is explicitly marked `c.`. The [JPL project announcement](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-goes-back-to-the-future-with-plans-for-a-mars-rover-in-2003-possible-second-rover-being-studied/) anchors the project to 2000; the film is not being asserted to provide a separately dated scene for each development year. The exact beginning of the documentary's development material is not independently established here. [NASA's Opportunity record](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity/) distinguishes last contact in June 2018 from end of mission in February 2019. Present-day interviews are excluded; CGI depictions of Mars operations are identified as reconstructions.

## Historical spine

Each of the 38 stops carries its source links in the data and generated page. NASA, ESA, ISRO, CNSA, AFI and the Smithsonian supply the principal evidence. Event dates are dates of achievements, not necessarily launch dates: Luna 9's 3 February 1966 landing, for example, differs from the launch listed in some mission chronologies. Within-year records are ordered by date.

The 1926 Goddard flight and the V-2/forced-labor stop establish context before the space race. Soviet milestones are retained independently of the American viewing selections. Later stops include Huygens, Mangalyaan, Rosetta, Chang'e-4 and Chandrayaan-3. Unconnected events explicitly state that no selected title covers them; date overlap never creates an automatic connection.

The ending is a deliberate historical boundary at a completed 2023 achievement, not a current mission-status statement. The guide omits many important subjects and nations and names those limits publicly. The 2019 alternate-history series *For All Mankind* is outside the scope; it must not be confused with the different 1989 Apollo documentary of the same name.
