# Next Turn: omitted-game research audit

Research date: **18 October 2026**. This follow-up screens **20 candidates**, researches **nine additions** in detail, and expands the guide from 14 to **23 games**. The original five horizon entries stay in place. This is a targeted search for gaps, not a claim that every suitable game has been found.

## What the search was trying to recover

The existing recommendations covered strategic conflict and efficient planning, but left gaps in classic majority games, spatial tile placement, territorial deck-building, short two-player competition, and heavier economic development. Candidates were checked against the supplied play log rather than selected solely by BGG rank. Blood Rage, Rising Sun and Azul suggest productive directions; the mixed response to The King is Dead and Woodcraft and the low Scout rating are useful counter-evidence. A dislike of one game does not establish a dislike of its entire mechanism or designer.

For the nine additions, publisher descriptions and available rules established the decision structure, player counts and expansion contents. BGG game entries supplied rounded rating and complexity snapshots; accessible review pages and indexed player comments supplied qualitative praise and complaints. European publisher or retailer listings supplied edition-specific euro price references. Some direct BGG pages were inaccessible, so indexed material was used: this limits the review sample and does not establish a community consensus. Individual comments are opinions, not measurements.

The eleven held candidates received a lighter screening. They are alternatives to revisit, not deeply researched rejections. There was no purchase, hands-on play, new user rating, or verification of a playable Essen demo for these additions.

## Added games and calculated scores

Scores use the existing editorial rubric: **Control 30%, Taste overlap 25%, Replay potential 15%, Group suitability 15%, Approachability 15%**. Each factor is 1–5. Multiply the weighted average by 20 and round half up to a whole number. These are comparative judgments, not measured probabilities. Confidence is medium: the games have established evidence, but personal enjoyment remains untested. Original game scores were preserved.

## Why each addition earned a place

### El Grande — 92/100

Fight over majorities with controlled card choices and no combat dice. An overlooked fit for your four- and five-player groups.

Blood Rage and Rising Sun suggest that contested territory matters to you; El Grande keeps that contest while making the action economy easier to inspect. Your power-card choice trades turn priority against reinforcements, creating a meaningful cost before you even choose an action. Four- and five-player sessions suit several counts in your play log.

**Main reasons it might miss:** Action cards can move or remove your pieces, and several opponents can target the leader. Low dice luck does not remove kingmaking or sharp reversals. This is a majority contest, not a growing engine of spectacular powers. Your 3/5 for The King is Dead makes confidence medium rather than automatic.

**Evidence check:** Accessible player comments praise engagement at four and five players, while warning that disruptive cards and leader-targeting can feel vindictive. That tension is relevant given your mixed response to The King is Dead.

**Price reference:** [€49.99](https://www.999games.nl/product/el-grande/8720289476403), 2023 edition · Dutch; checked 2026-10-18. Publisher reference is marked out of assortment, so this is a price reference rather than an available offer.

**Expansion guidance:** The current-edition recommendation is for the base game. An old Big Box listing is not evidence that its content is included in a 2023 box. [New power-card and action-card mini-expansions](https://www.999games.nl/product/el-grande/8720289476403): Adds optional power and action choices to vary the classic rules. Included in the 2023 edition; start with standard cards. [Legacy Big Box expansions](https://www.999games.nl/product/el-grande-big-box/8717249197584): Older bundles collect Grandissimo, King & Intrigant and Grand Inquisitor & Colonies, changing cards, regions and action choices. An older Big Box is a different product. Check component sizes and exact rules before mixing with 2023 editions.

### Babylonia — 90/100

Azul-like placement discipline meets contested trade networks, with a larger payoff for planning connections.

Your 4/5 Azul rating suggests you may enjoy a compact spatial action with consequences beyond the immediate placement. Ticket to Ride provides a second connection: blocking and network continuity matter, without dice-based battles. Temple rewards and connected-city scoring reward timing rather than simply taking the largest visible number.

**Main reasons it might miss:** The theme is thin, and the scoring routes need a careful teach. It will not reproduce Blood Rage’s dramatic power growth. Blocking can close routes permanently. The game may feel understated before players learn to read its networks.

**Evidence check:** Player comments describe a tension between early income and larger networks, and praise blocking and quick turns. Some find the tension too mild. These individual reactions support a trial, rather than a guarantee of depth for your group.

**Price reference:** [€48.88](https://www.spellenvariant.nl/babylonia), Babylonia · English; checked 2026-10-18. Discounted listing from €58.88; currently unavailable in the accessible shop snapshot.

**Expansion guidance:** No official gameplay expansion or paid promo was verified in the publisher material reviewed. The core box already contains variable ziggurat rewards.

### The Quest for El Dorado — 89/100

Build a deck for a visible race course, then time your purchases and blocking. A lighter way to test deck-building.

Splendor’s investment timing and Ticket to Ride’s route competition meet in a physical race rather than an abstract points engine. The terrain tells you what your deck needs; you can assess whether a purchase advances a plan instead of relying on theme or novelty. Changing courses and a broad card market offer repeat-play decisions within a relatively accessible ruleset.

**Main reasons it might miss:** Shuffled hands can arrive in the wrong order; this is a moderate-luck recommendation, not a fully deterministic puzzle. It is lighter than The White Castle, and setup can feel long for the weight. Tight finishes can hinge on draw timing.

**Evidence check:** BGG comments praise the combination of deck development and positional blocking, but also describe light interaction or an anticlimactic finish. One October 2026 comment flags setup relative to game weight.

**Price reference:** [€42.00](https://en.lautapelit.fi/product/30541/quest-for-el-dorado), Lautapelit.fi edition · includes English cards; checked 2026-10-18. Publisher shop price; shipping to your delivery country is extra.

### Tyrants of the Underdark — 86/100

Buy the tools for territorial control, then decide which cards to retire for points. A direct bridge from development to conflict.

Blood Rage suggests that developing a distinctive toolkit is most satisfying when it has consequences on a shared board. Map control gives your card engine an immediate tactical purpose rather than merely producing more currency. Promoting cards to your inner circle creates a trade-off between keeping an effect and banking its higher endgame value.

**Main reasons it might miss:** Card draws and market timing can swing your tactical options. Deck-building may be a weaker taste signal than territory control. The dark map and cardboard troops are less inviting than the mechanisms, and direct assassinations can feel harsh.

**Evidence check:** Player comments repeatedly connect deck-building to meaningful map tactics and describe variety from the paired market decks. Other comments criticize token visibility and the need to separate the market decks after play.

**Price reference:** [€58.95](https://www.philibertnet.com/en/gale-force-nine/104303-tyrants-of-the-underdark-updated-edition-9781638840008.html), 2021 updated edition · English; checked 2026-10-18. European retailer reference, with shipping extra.

Included in the 2021 edition. The separately sold expansion was for older copies.

### Ankh: Gods of Egypt — 84/100

Asymmetric gods, visible action timing and card-driven battles fit your conflict tastes, with an unusually important multiplayer caveat.

This is a relevant follow-up to Blood Rage and Rising Sun because of its card combat and power development, not merely the shared designer. Choosing an action also advances the timing of events, making tempo itself a contested resource. Deterministic battle resolution rewards anticipating an opponent’s card rather than hoping for a dice result.

**Main reasons it might miss:** In games with three or more players, the two lowest gods merge partway through. Sharing control and the associated catch-up rules may conflict with your desire for agency. Players can be eliminated by failing a devotion threshold. A two-player game avoids the merger but is a different social experience from Blood Rage.

**Evidence check:** Comments praise event timing and battle-card decisions, while disagreeing sharply about the merger and strength of the god powers. The fit is conditional: try the actual multiplayer rules before buying a large bundle.

**Price reference:** [€89.90](https://www.philibertnet.com/en/cool-mini-or-not/99858-ankh-les-dieux-d-egypte-3558380087175.html), Original CMON base game · French; checked 2026-10-18. Language-specific European reference. English second-edition listings are separate products.

### 7 Wonders Duel — 84/100

A natural follow-up to Splendor Duel: develop your economy while watching two sudden-death threats.

Your 4/5 Splendor Duel result makes a tense development duel worth testing alongside Santorini’s more abstract contest. Military and science can end the game immediately, so an efficient points engine is not enough on its own. Choosing a card can uncover your opponent’s next choice; timing and denial are linked rather than separate puzzles.

**Main reasons it might miss:** Face-down cards create reveal luck, and turn-order parity can sometimes force an unattractive choice. This may be more frustrating than Splendor Duel’s open resource board. It serves only two players, so it does not replace your three- to five-player nights.

**Evidence check:** A detailed BGG review rates the interaction and strategic depth highly while retaining a luck caveat. Treat that as one reviewer’s assessment; the hidden-card layout is the main mechanism to test against your preferences.

**Price reference:** [€24.27](https://www.bol.com/nl/nl/p/7-wonders-duel-bordspel/9200000062721097/), 7 Wonders Duel · Dutch; checked 2026-10-18. Discounted bol listing from €26.99 RRP; English editions may differ in price.

**Expansion guidance:** The original 7 Wonders expansions do not fit Duel. Learn Duel before choosing whether you want Pantheon’s tempo options or Agora’s extra political system. ### Five Tribes — 83/100

A shared-board optimization puzzle for your Azul and White Castle side, with a warning about recalculating every turn.

Like Azul, a visible shared pool can hide a rich placement puzzle; here picking up and distributing meeples creates the action. Bidding for turn order links the value of a move to what you must pay to secure it. Djinn powers and several scoring paths offer a different tactical optimization problem from The White Castle.

**Main reasons it might miss:** Other players can remake the board before your turn, so analysis paralysis and downtime are substantial risks. It leans tactical rather than toward a stable long-term plan. Your Woodcraft comment makes another optimization game a cautious addition.

**Evidence check:** The core rules expose a large shared-board search. A BGG discussion of game length specifically flags Five Tribes for making advance planning difficult when the board changes. This caution is central to its lower fit score.

**Price reference:** [€53.99](https://despelletjesvrienden.nl/products/five-tribes-bordspel-eng), Five Tribes · English; checked 2026-10-18. Discounted shop reference from €59.99; stock labels in the accessible page are mixed, so recheck availability.

[The Artisans of Naqala](https://www.daysofwonder.com/game/five-tribes-the-artisans-of-naqala/): Adds a sixth tribe, crafted items, workshops and mountain / chasm restrictions. Requires Five Tribes. [Whims of the Sultan](https://www.daysofwonder.com/game/five-tribes-whims-of-the-sultan/): Adds a fifth player, fabulous cities and objectives requested by the Sultan. ### Brass: Birmingham — 84/100

A heavier experiment in shared incentives: your rival’s coal mine or railway can become part of your plan.

Your White Castle and Splendor ratings suggest you may enjoy investments that change which later actions become efficient. **Main reasons it might miss:** A major learning commitment: coal connectivity, iron, beer, card restrictions and the two eras are easy to confuse on a first play. It is longer and less immediately legible than your lighter favorites. High BGG popularity does not resolve that fit risk.

### Gaia Project — 86/100

A deliberate stretch toward asymmetric engines and almost complete information, with a substantial complexity cost.

Your desire for control is strongly served by visible scoring rounds, known resource costs and no dice-driven action resolution. Different factions and research paths offer the power development you liked in Blood Rage in an economic form. The map and shared power actions prevent the engine puzzle from being entirely solitary.

**Main reasons it might miss:** Its 4.40 complexity is a large step above much of your history. A first play can feel like learning several resource economies at once. Interaction is largely competition for opportunities and proximity benefits; it will not replace a confrontational Blood Rage session.

**Evidence check:** An enthusiastic BGG review praises the interlocking systems, while other player discussions describe information overload and teaching friction. Its high control score is balanced by the lowest approachability score among the new additions.

**Price reference:** [€69.90 MSRP](https://www.feuerland-spiele.de/spiele/gaia-project/), Gaia Project · German edition; checked 2026-10-18. Publisher German RRP, not a live English-edition retail quote.

## Limits and follow-up priorities

- Try **El Grande at four or five**, **Babylonia**, and **The Quest for El Dorado** first to test the strongest new gaps without beginning with the heaviest games.
- Treat **Ankh's merger** ([official rules, pages 25–27](https://www.cmon.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Ankh_Rulebook.pdf)) and **Five Tribes' analysis time** as explicit trial questions. Neither is erased by a good aggregate score.
- Use **7 Wonders Duel** for exactly two players. Tyrants uses the **2021 compilation** entry; statistics and included expansion decks must not be confused with the 2016 box.
- **Brass** and **Gaia Project** are deliberate weight experiments. Their fit scores do not imply a quick teach or a guaranteed match.
