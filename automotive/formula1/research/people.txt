1950|Juan Manuel Fangio|Juan Manuel Fangio|The benchmark before Schumacher|Five titles in the 1950s, won with four constructors. His championship total is the record Schumacher will eventually surpass.
1955|Stirling Moss|Stirling Moss|Greatness without a world title|A leading rival and team-mate of Fangio. Remember him when the timeline separates outstanding performances from championship totals.
1959|Jack Brabham|Jack Brabham|Driver, constructor, champion|A champion with Cooper who will later win the title in a car carrying his own name. His story links the rear-engine revolution to the independent teams.
1962|Jim Clark|Jim Clark|The driver at the heart of Lotus|Clark’s precision meets Chapman’s inventive, often radical cars. Clark wins titles in 1963 and 1965; Chapman’s Lotus later pioneers ground effect.
1969|Jackie Stewart|Jackie Stewart|A champion who pushes for safety|Three titles and a sustained campaign to improve the conditions in which drivers race. His influence matters again when the timeline reaches later safety crises.
1975|Niki Lauda|Niki Lauda|The analytical champion|Ferrari’s technical leader, the survivor of the 1976 Nürburgring fire, and later a key figure at Mercedes. His story will return in both films and title fights.
1976|James Hunt|James Hunt|Lauda’s rival, later a familiar voice|The 1976 champion is remembered for a contrasting public persona and later for television commentary. The film Rush dramatises their rivalry; the archive supplies the racing record.
1980|Bernie Ecclestone|Bernie Ecclestone|The commercial power broker|A former Brabham team owner who becomes central to F1’s commercial expansion and television business. His story runs across decades rather than one championship.
1982|Alain Prost|Alain Prost|The calculating rival|Four championships, a celebrated rivalry with Senna and a final title at Williams. Look beyond the nickname “The Professor”: tactical judgement can still produce intense conflict.
1984|Ayrton Senna|Ayrton Senna|Speed, conviction and confrontation|He debuts with Toleman in 1984, wins three championships with McLaren and becomes Prost’s defining rival. His death at Imola in 1994 changes the sport.
1986|Nigel Mansell|Nigel Mansell|The long road to a championship|Repeated near misses precede the dominant 1992 Williams title. His duels with Piquet and Senna explain why the eventual breakthrough carries so much weight.
1991|Michael Schumacher|Michael Schumacher|The next era begins here|A Jordan debut leads to Benetton titles, then a long Ferrari rebuilding project. Seven championships and a Mercedes comeback make him the central thread of this watchlist.
1992|Adrian Newey|Adrian Newey|The designer behind successive eras|His cars win championships at Williams, McLaren and Red Bull. Watch for the connection between aerodynamic ideas and the drivers who make them famous.
1994|Damon Hill|Damon Hill|Schumacher’s first major title rival|Graham Hill’s son becomes Williams’ team leader after Senna’s death. He loses the 1994 title by one point, wins in 1996 and later gives Jordan its first victory.
1996|Ross Brawn|Ross Brawn|The technical director behind Ferrari’s rebuild|Todt leads the team and Brawn directs the technical operation around Schumacher. Brawn later heads the extraordinary 2009 championship team.
1997|Mika Häkkinen|Mika Häkkinen|Schumacher’s McLaren counterpart|His first win comes at Jerez in 1997; titles follow in 1998 and 1999. The rivalry offers a different tone from the collisions that decide other championships.
2001|Fernando Alonso|Fernando Alonso|The driver who ends the Ferrari streak|He arrives in F1 with Minardi in 2001 and becomes Renault’s champion in 2005 and 2006. His later title battles with Vettel and Hamilton span very different teams.
2001|Kimi Räikkönen|Kimi Räikkönen|From Sauber newcomer to Ferrari champion|Fast-tracked into F1 in 2001, he becomes a McLaren title contender and wins the 2007 championship with Ferrari. His 2005 Suzuka win is a key stop along the way.
2007|Lewis Hamilton|Lewis Hamilton|A rookie who immediately challenges for the title|Hamilton begins with McLaren in 2007, wins his first title in 2008 and later builds a championship dynasty at Mercedes. His move to Ferrari begins in 2025.
2007|Sebastian Vettel|Sebastian Vettel|A future four-time champion|A first win with Toro Rosso precedes four consecutive championships with Red Bull. His later Ferrari years and farewell documentary add another side to the story.
2009|Jenson Button|Jenson Button|The experienced driver in an unlikely champion team|Years of mixed machinery precede Brawn’s 2009 breakthrough. His 2011 Canada win later shows how he can thrive when a race refuses to follow a predictable script.
2010|Nico Rosberg|Nico Rosberg|Hamilton’s eventual team-mate rival|He joins Mercedes for its works-team return alongside Schumacher. His championship rivalry with Hamilton builds over several seasons before the 2016 finale.
2013|Toto Wolff|Toto Wolff|Mercedes’ team leader|A central figure in Mercedes’ hybrid-era success and in managing the Hamilton–Rosberg rivalry. The team’s dominance involves leadership as well as engine and chassis design.
2014|Daniel Ricciardo|Daniel Ricciardo|The late-braking opportunist|He wins races in his first Red Bull season and becomes known for committed overtaking. Remember him before Baku 2017 and McLaren’s 2021 Monza victory.
2015|Max Verstappen|Max Verstappen|A teenager who becomes a title protagonist|He debuts with Toro Rosso at 17, wins on his Red Bull debut in 2016 and fights Hamilton for the 2021 title. His later championships are part of the ground-effect era.
2018|Charles Leclerc|Charles Leclerc|A new Ferrari contender approaches|A Sauber debut precedes his move to Ferrari in 2019. His speed and rivalry with Verstappen become recurring themes as the older champions face a new generation.
2019|Lando Norris|Lando Norris|McLaren’s long-term bet|He debuts in 2019, takes his first win in 2024 and becomes champion in 2025. The early introduction lets you follow the years of development before the breakthrough.
2019|George Russell|George Russell (racing driver)|From Williams to the Mercedes seat|He begins at Williams, nearly wins while substituting at Mercedes in Sakhir 2020, and later joins the team full-time. He wins the first race under the 2026 rules.
2023|Oscar Piastri|Oscar Piastri|The rookie who soon enters a title fight|Piastri joins McLaren in 2023 and becomes a contender alongside Norris and Verstappen in 2025. Team-mate competition is about to become central to McLaren’s story.
2025|Kimi Antonelli|Andrea Kimi Antonelli|A new Mercedes generation|The Italian rookie enters F1 with Mercedes in 2025. His first Grand Prix victory comes in China in 2026; the current season is still unfolding at this page’s cutoff.
1950|Enzo Ferrari|Enzo Ferrari|The founder behind the red cars|Ferrari enters the championship in its first season. The founder’s identity, the road-car business and the racing team remain intertwined through the decades ahead.
1978|Frank Williams|Frank Williams (Formula One)|The independent constructor|His partnership with engineer Patrick Head creates a team that wins titles across several eras. Remember the organisation before the Mansell, Hill and Villeneuve championships.
1991|Eddie Jordan|Eddie Jordan|The team owner who gives Schumacher his start|Jordan’s team arrives in F1 in 1991. It becomes an important home for emerging drivers and eventually wins at Spa in 1998.
2005|Christian Horner|Christian Horner|Red Bull’s founding team principal|He leads the new Red Bull operation from 2005 through its Vettel and Verstappen championship eras. Introduced here before those successes rather than at the moment they arrive.
1962|Colin Chapman|Colin Chapman|The inventor behind Lotus|A constructor whose ideas repeatedly change the shape of F1: the monocoque, aerodynamic experiments and ground effect. The speed of innovation also brings difficult questions about reliability and safety.
1963|Bruce McLaren|Bruce McLaren|The driver who builds a lasting team|A Grand Prix winner with Cooper who establishes his own racing team in 1963. McLaren becomes a central name in the championships of Fittipaldi, Lauda, Prost, Senna, Häkkinen and Hamilton.
1981|Ron Dennis|Ron Dennis|The leader of McLaren’s new chapter|He leads McLaren’s transformation in the early 1980s. The operation becomes home to the Prost–Senna rivalry, Häkkinen’s titles and Hamilton’s arrival decades later.
1993|Jean Todt|Jean Todt|The team principal rebuilding Ferrari|He joins Ferrari in 1993 and assembles the organisation that later wins with Schumacher. Introduced before the titles, because the management rebuild precedes the results.
