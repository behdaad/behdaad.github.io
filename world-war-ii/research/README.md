## Main-plot date decisions

- **The Gathering Storm:** the International Churchill Society's review explicitly describes a jump after the central 1934–1936 narrative to the outbreak of war. The brief 1939 return-to-office coda is excluded. Broader listings calling the film “1934–1939” do not satisfy the requested main-plot rule.
- **City of Life and Death:** the film begins with Nanjing's fall in December 1937 and follows the occupation and atrocities into early 1938. The cross-year label follows the period described in the cited film review, rather than claiming two full years or day-level precision. IMDb often abbreviates the premise to 1937.
- **Schindler’s List:** occupied Poland from 1939 to liberation in 1945; the present-day graveside epilogue is excluded.
- **Dunkirk:** all three intercut time scales remain in May–June 1940.
- **Das Boot:** the patrol is in autumn/winter 1941; the 1981 film is distinct from the 1985 recut and later television franchise.
- **Tora! Tora! Tora!:** Pearl Harbor's 1941 planning/attack is the main story. The short earlier command and diplomatic setup is excluded, rather than extending its coverage back to 1939.
- **The Pacific:** central enlistment/family scenes begin after Pearl Harbor in December 1941. Combat runs from Guadalcanal in 1942 through 1945. Episode 10 contains substantial homecoming/readjustment material, including Sledge's return six months after V-J Day, so 1946 is included. This is not a decades-later framing epilogue. Later interviews/biographical captions do not extend the range. Sources include HBO's series description, the episode-one plot chronology and the final episode's IMDb plot record, which also reproduces HBO Publicity's synopsis.
- **Enemy at the Gates:** the principal sniper narrative is set in 1942; the childhood prologue and brief hospital reunion two months later, after Stalingrad's fall in 1943, are excluded. The event note gives the full 1942–1943 battle dates separately. The range is an editorial main-plot judgement, not a claim that every scene is in 1942. The duel is contested and the film substantially dramatises Soviet warfare.
- **Patton:** AFI identifies the first action as Kasserine Pass, 1943; the narrative continues to the end of the European war. The stylised opening speech does not expand the range.
- **Band of Brothers:** HBO dates Georgia training to 1942. The central story continues through 1945; later veteran interviews and biographical captions are excluded. Its liberation episode concerns a Dachau satellite camp, not Auschwitz. Viewing order groups the later western campaign after Patton rather than strictly sorting all same-year series starts.
- **Downfall:** the main story is April–May 1945. The short 1942 secretary-recruitment prologue, later documentary material and end captions are excluded.
- **Grave of the Fireflies:** the main civilian story and its opening belong to 1945. The closing modern skyline does not extend the bar. Kobe's conventional firebombing must not be confused with the atomic attacks.

## Coverage choices

The route begins with Nazi dictatorship and rearmament, then establishes that the Asian war was underway before Europe's conventional September 1939 starting point. Military turning points sit alongside occupation, genocide and civilian survival. The two series earn their time commitment by connecting successive campaigns rather than repeating a single battle.

Patton supplies Tunisia, Sicily and later European operations in one selection. It does not show El Alamein, Torch or the mainland Italian campaign. Dunkirk's air scenes are contextual links to the subsequent Battle of Britain, not a claim of depicting that campaign. The Pacific depicts Guadalcanal, not the carrier battle of Midway. The atomic bombings and Soviet entry are explained as context, without pretending Grave of the Fireflies depicts either.

The principal omissions include the wider Eastern Front, resistance movements, strategic bombing in Europe, Burma and Southeast Asia, and colonial experiences. The page names these limits. No streaming availability has been researched or promised.
