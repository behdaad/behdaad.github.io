## Date and context decisions

- **37 Days:** BBC Studios describes the three-part series as the crisis from 28 June to 4 August 1914. A BBC project guide lists three 60-minute episodes. Its 1913 background connection is explicitly contextual; no central 1913 story is claimed.
- **Joyeux Noël:** production and title-page synopses centre it on Christmas 1914. Its outbreak-of-war setup belongs to that year; the short undated disciplinary aftermath does not establish another substantial story year.
- **Gallipoli:** Australian Screen identifies the opening in Western Australia in 1915 and the climax at the Nek that August. This is not a depiction of the April landings or the later evacuation. Its command-blame distortion is noted using Australian Screen's curator commentary.
- **Lawrence:** AFI starts the central plot in 1916 and follows it to Damascus; the National Army Museum dates that campaign endpoint to October 1918. The 1935 death/memorial framing is excluded. The restored runtime comes from IMDb technical specifications, rather than shorter-cut listings.
- **1917:** IMDb dates the mission's beginning to 6 April; the narrative runs into the following morning. The historical withdrawal is documented by the National Army Museum and discussed with WWI Museum curators in TIME. Its 1916 event connection is labelled “Later context”.
- **All Quiet:** Netflix's production announcement dates Paul's enlistment to spring 1917; the central story then jumps to November 1918. Netflix's account of the adaptation confirms the final-days emphasis and Armistice ending. Both years are retained because enlistment is a substantial first act, not a framing scene. Historian Jonathan Boff's [University of Birmingham commentary](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/news/2023/not-all-quiet-on-the-western-front) supports the distinction between the film's imagery and 1918 combat, including its invented German last-minute attack. Historical fighting continued until the ceasefire; the fictional general and attack are not presented as documented events.

## Editorial limits

This is a short route through engaging drama, not comprehensive coverage or a ranking by score alone. A political miniseries and a Middle Eastern epic add perspectives that another trench film would not. The 1930 All Quiet and Paths of Glory are strong alternatives, but adding them would duplicate ground and exceed the six-title budget.

The timeline retains Verdun, the Somme, US entry and Russia's revolutions because a beginner needs them. No selected title is represented as depicting those events. The US/Russia stop has no recommendation, while the 1916 note links to later Western Front context. The Eastern Front, naval war, Africa and many civilian/colonial perspectives remain gaps, stated on the page. No streaming availability has been verified or promised.
