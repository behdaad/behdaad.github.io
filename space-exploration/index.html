Space exploration

13 titles · ≈ 37H 45M if you watch everything

About the selection & IMDb ratings

Thirteen choices: four dramatic films, one dramatic miniseries and eight documentary films, series or episodes. The expanded route adds SpaceX’s development and Inspiration4, Voyager’s later ground team, and Dutch perspectives through Wubbo Ockels and André Kuipers. First Man and Apollo 13 remain optional close-ups alongside From the Earth to the Moon; The Farthest and It’s Quieter in the Twilight offer complementary Voyager eras. Watching everything is about 37 hours 45 minutes, using an hour per Apollo-series episode and the editions described in the title notes. IMDb scores were checked on 30 October 2026 through publicly indexed pages; a dash marks an unverified score, not zero. Scores measure reception, not reliability. Documentary bars describe the historical subject or observed operations; interviews and brief archive do not extend them. A long envelope can contain jumps, and c. marks approximate boundaries.

HOW TO READ THIS

One story can cross many years.

The top chart compares every film against the same year axis. In the event timeline, each film occupies a parallel lane and its coloured bar stretches across its main-story years. Separate periods leave a gap. Event dots align with their historical cards, including contextual connections outside a film’s main-story span. Select any movie name or span to open its details without leaving the timeline. The modal includes its IMDb score, story-year strip, historical caveats and sources. Coloured cells mark main-story coverage and pale cells mark years outside it. Gaps stay visible. Brief prologues, framing interviews and later-life epilogues are excluded. IMDb scores describe audience reception, not historical accuracy.

The rest of the picture

This is a selective route, not a complete history or a ranking. Dutch space activity appears through astronomy satellites, ESTEC, CubeSats, atmospheric instruments and astronauts: these are distinct national and international contributions, rather than a single independent Dutch launch program. ESTEC is an ESA center in the Netherlands; TROPOMI is a Dutch instrument on a European satellite. The viewing choices still lean American and European. Japanese exploration, the full Soviet/Russian and Chinese programs, space law, commercial constellations and many failed missions need a broader guide. Unconnected stops mark gaps in the screen selection. SpaceX films have close participant access and are read alongside mission and regulatory sources. Space fantasy and alternate-history series, including the 2019 For All Mankind, remain outside this historical selection.

The expanded historical spine ends with completed SpaceX milestones in 2024, including Polaris Dawn and the first Super Heavy tower catch. These later stops are context beyond the selected SpaceX films’ story years, not claims that those films depict them. This is not a live mission tracker or a claim that exploration stopped in 2024. Agency, university, museum, broadcaster and regulatory records provide the historical checks; no streaming availability or subtitle support is guaranteed.
