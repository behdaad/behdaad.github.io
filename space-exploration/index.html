About the selection & IMDb ratings

Eight choices: four dramatic films, one 12-part dramatic series, two feature documentaries and one four-part documentary series. Start with The Right Stuff, then choose the Apollo perspectives you want: First Man and Apollo 13 overlap the broader From the Earth to the Moon. Watching everything is about 28 hours 45 minutes, including roughly an hour per Apollo-series episode. IMDb scores were checked on 30 October 2026 through publicly indexed IMDb pages; cached snapshots can lag and scores can change. Scores measure audience reception, not historical reliability. Documentary bars cover the historical subject, excluding present-day interviews; a long mission envelope does not imply that every year appears on screen.

HOW TO READ THIS

One story can cross many years.

The top chart compares every film against the same year axis. In the event timeline, each film occupies a parallel lane and its coloured bar stretches across its main-story years. Separate periods leave a gap. Event dots align with their historical cards, including contextual connections outside a film’s main-story span. A connection labelled “Context” or “Background” does not mean the film depicts that event.

Select any movie name or span to open its details without leaving the timeline. The modal includes its IMDb score, story-year strip, historical caveats and sources. Coloured cells mark main-story coverage and pale cells mark years outside it. Gaps stay visible. Brief prologues, framing interviews and later-life epilogues are excluded. IMDb scores describe audience reception, not historical accuracy.

The rest of the picture

This is a selective route, not a complete history or a ranking. The screen choices lean heavily toward American missions; the historical spine retains Soviet, European, Indian and Chinese milestones even where no selected title covers them. Japanese exploration, the full Soviet/Russian program, Chinese human spaceflight, reusable-launch development, space law, Earth observation, commercial satellites and many failed missions need a broader guide. Read the unconnected events as gaps in the viewing route, not gaps in history. Space fantasy and alternate-history series, including the 2019 For All Mankind, are outside this historical selection.

The timeline deliberately ends with a completed 2023 milestone; it is not a live mission tracker or a claim that exploration stopped there. Lunar landing locations, mission endings and interstellar boundaries are described carefully rather than copied from promotional shorthand. Film and documentary reconstructions remain interpretations; the linked agency and museum records provide the historical checks.
